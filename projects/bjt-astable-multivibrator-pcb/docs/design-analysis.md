# Design analysis and assumptions

This note restates the submitted report's calculation path and corrects its tolerance interpretation. It does not change the archived circuit or claim a new SPICE run. [Calculated values](../analysis/calculated_values.csv) are generated by `python analysis/design_calculations.py` using only the Python standard library.

## 1. Timing component choice

For equal timing branches, the first-order approximation is:

$$t_{1/2}\approx0.69R_BC,\qquad f\approx\frac{1}{1.38R_BC}.$$

With `RB = 47 kΩ` and target `f = 3 Hz`, the required capacitance is about **5.14 µF**. The chosen **5.1 µF** gives **3.023 Hz** (reported as 3.02 Hz), with a half-period of about 0.165 s. This uses the rounded 0.69 timing factor from the report. Availability, tolerance, voltage rating, and footprint must be confirmed for a purchasable capacitor.

## 2. Junction-drop estimate and reported waveform

For the pre-LED circuit, assume `VCC = 9 V`, `VBE(on) = 0.7 V`, and `VCE(sat) = 0.2 V`. The coupled negative step gives `VB,min ≈ 0.7 - (9 - 0.2) = -8.1 V`. Charging toward the supply gives:

$$t_{1/2}\approx R_BC\ln\left(\frac{V_{CC}-V_{B,min}}{V_{CC}-V_{BE(on)}}\right).$$

This evaluates to about **2.886 Hz** (2.89 Hz rounded). It accounts for assumed junction drops but omits switching dynamics, loading, parasitics, and breakdown, so it should not be called an exact circuit solution.

The report reads switching times approximately at 1.08, 1.25, 1.42, 1.60, 1.77, and 1.94 s from its plotted waveform. Averaging five half-cycles gives `(1.94−1.08)/5 = 0.172 s`, hence `f ≈ 2.907 Hz`, or **2.91 Hz**. Its agreement with the junction-drop approximation is about 0.7% using the rounded reported values. The screenshot limits the precision of this comparison; it is not raw sampled waveform data.

## 3. Timing tolerances: nominal compliance is insufficient

Under the same first-order equation:

| Assumption | Frequency range | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Nominal R and C | 3.023 Hz | Within 2.4–3.6 Hz |
| C ±20%; R nominal | 2.519–3.779 Hz | Upper endpoint exceeds target |
| R ±5% and C ±20%, aligned extremes | 2.399–3.978 Hz | Both limits can be exceeded |

These are analytical worst-case component corners in the stated approximation, not a probability distribution or a SPICE tolerance sweep. The original report's statement that the full ±20% capacitor range fits within the specification was too strong. Even its rounded junction-corrected upper endpoint of 3.61 Hz is slightly above 3.6 Hz. Tighter components or a changed nominal design would need a new evaluated revision.

## 4. LED branch estimate and source-model distinction

The final PCB inserts red LEDs into the collector paths and replaces the ideal source with a battery connector. Using assumed `VF = 2.0 V` and `VCE(sat) = 0.2 V`:

$$I_{LED}\approx\frac{9-2-0.2}{1000}=6.8\;\mathrm{mA}.$$

This is a nominal estimate, not a measured LED current or a guarantee for all red LEDs. LED loading changes the collector swing and capacitor charging path. The pre-LED 2.91 Hz simulation therefore cannot establish the final board's frequency.

The native schematic embeds `.MODEL QNPN NPN` with no explicit transistor parameter list. Q1/Q2 are generic `NPN_TO92-EBC` devices. The report lists 2N3904 as an example candidate; neither the archive nor the report establishes a fitted production part. [BOM](../BOM.csv) distinguishes design values, reported ratings, and selection status.

## 5. Physical questions to resolve

- **Reverse base-emitter stress:** the pre-LED approximation reaches −8.1 V. The candidate onsemi 2N3904 datasheet specifies a 6 V emitter-base maximum rating. Breakdown must not be treated as a normal timing clamp or as evidence of safe operation. The final LED circuit needs a device-specific stress assessment and any protection change needs a new simulation.
- **Saturation assumptions:** active-region current gain alone does not prove a chosen saturation voltage. The candidate datasheet specifies saturation conditions at explicit collector and base currents; compare those with the actual drive.
- **Polarized capacitors:** verify voltage across each capacitor throughout startup and steady state, and match its polarity and lead spacing to the selected part. A static collector/base voltage comparison does not establish polarity during the whole cycle.
- **Manufacturing rules:** a DRC result is relative to configured rules. The later warnings require individual review rather than blanket approval.
